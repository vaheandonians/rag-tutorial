Harbor City Transit Authority - Annual Service Review 2025 (Excerpt)
(Fictional city and authority, created for teaching purposes.)

Overview

The Harbor City Transit Authority operates the bus, tram and ferry network of Harbor City, a coastal city of about 650,000 residents. It carried 182 million passengers in 2025, a new record that surpassed the pre-pandemic peak for the first time.

Network

The authority runs 64 bus lines, 9 tram lines and 3 ferry routes. In 2025 it converted its entire tram fleet to regenerative braking, which returns energy to the overhead lines when trams slow down. The conversion cut the tram network's electricity consumption by 18 percent.

The ferry routes connect the old harbor with the two islands in the bay. They are the authority's smallest service by passenger numbers but the most popular with tourists. In summer the ferries run every 15 minutes; in winter they run every 40 minutes.

Finances

The authority's operating budget for 2025 was 410 million dollars. Fare revenue covered 38 percent of operating costs; the remainder came from a city subsidy and a regional transport fund. A single ticket costs 2.80 dollars, and a monthly pass costs 79 dollars. Residents over 65 travel free of charge.

Reliability

On-time performance, defined as arriving no more than three minutes late, reached 91 percent for trams and 84 percent for buses. The authority attributes the gap mainly to traffic congestion on the bus corridors along the waterfront. Dedicated bus lanes on Quay Street are planned for 2026 and are expected to raise bus punctuality by around five percentage points.
