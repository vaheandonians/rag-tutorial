## Notes to Financial Statements

Years Ended December 31, 2019 and 2018

### Note 1 - Nature of Operations and Summary of Significant Accounting Policies

#### Nature of Operations

The Employer Group, Inc. (“Company”) is a professional employer organization that contractually assumes legal authority for the workforce of various small businesses located primarily in the Mid-Western and Western areas of the United States. As employer of record for their workforces, the Company provides a variety of services including benefits and benefits administration, payroll and payroll management, legal compliance with statutory employment and human resources services and management. The Company also offers payroll processing and human resource services to customers without being the employer of record.

#### Cash and Cash Equivalents

For purposes of the statements of cash flows, the Company considers all highly liquid investments with initial maturities of three months or less to be cash equivalents. At December 31, 2019 and 2018, cash totaling \$145,276 and \$145,058, respectively, was restricted as collateral on a new building for the Company.

#### Accounts Receivable and Credit Policies

Accounts receivable are uncollateralized customer obligations due under normal trade terms generally requiring payment within 30 days from the invoice date. Transactions derive from signed contractual agreements with customers. Company policy allows for but does not require charging customers a finance charge of 1.5 percent per month. Therefore, interest is not accrued on any accounts receivable.

The Company continually evaluates the need for a valuation allowance by reviewing specific customer history and existing economic conditions. Accounts receivable are shown net of an allowance for doubtful accounts of \$10,000 at December 31, 2019 and 2018. Accounts are written off after all collection efforts have been exhausted and management determines the account will not be collected.

#### Revenue and Unbilled Revenue Receivable

The Company accounts for its revenues in accordance with Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) Accounting Standards Codification (ASC) 605-45, Principal Agent Considerations. The Company's revenues earned in transactions where they are the employer of record are derived from its gross billings, which are based on the payroll cost of its worksite employees, a markup computed as a percentage of the payroll cost, and a flat fee for each employee. The gross billings are invoiced concurrently with each periodic payroll of its worksite employees. Revenues are recognized ratably over the payroll period as worksite employees perform their service at the client worksite. Revenues that have been recognized but not invoiced are included in unbilled revenue receivable on the Company's financial statements.

Revenues carried in transactions where the Company is not the employer of record are computed on a percentage of the payroll processed for the customer.

#### Fixed Assets

Fixed assets are stated at original cost, less accumulated depreciation. Major expenditures for fixed assets are capitalized. Maintenance, repairs, and minor renewals are expensed as incurred. When assets are retired or otherwise disposed of, their costs and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the accounts and resulting gains or losses are included in income. Depreciation is provided for in amounts sufficient to relate the costs of the assets to operations over their estimated useful lives as follows:

#### Impairment of Long-lived Assets

The Company periodically reviews the carrying value of its long-lived assets, including fixed assets, whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. An impairment loss would be recognized to the extent fair value of a long-lived asset is less than the carrying amount. Fair value is determined based upon the estimated future cash inflows attributable to the asset less estimated future cash outflows. To date, there have been no such losses.

#### Income Taxes

The Company has elected under the Internal Revenue Code to be taxed as a Subchapter S corporation. In lieu of corporate income taxes, the shareholders of a Subchapter S corporation are taxed on their proportionate share of the Company's taxable income. Therefore, no provision or liability for income taxes has been included in these financial statements.

The Company is subject to various state taxes. Tax expense, included in operating expenses, for the years ended December 31, 2019 and 2018 was \$7,754 and \$7,552, respectively.

The Company assesses the likelihood of uncertain tax positions each year. The tax effects from an uncertain tax position can be recognized in the financial statements only if the position is more likely than not to be sustained on audit, based on the technical merits of the position. The Company reviewed all tax positions for which the statute of limitations remained open. The Company files a federal income tax return and files a tax return in the state of Wisconsin. The Company's U.S. federal income tax returns for years 2015 and prior, and the Company's Wisconsin income tax return for years 2014 and prior, are no longer subject to examination by tax authorities. As of December 31, 2019, the Company has no unrecognized tax benefits that, if recognized, would affect the Company's effective income tax rate over the next 12 months and therefore, have also not recorded any interest or penalties related to unrecognized tax benefits.

#### Advertising Costs

Advertising costs are expensed as incurred. Advertising expense was \$79,801 and \$84,319 for the years ended December 31, 2019 and 2018, respectively.

#### Use of Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during the reporting period. Actual results could differ from those estimates.

#### Compensated Absences

Employees of the Company are entitled to paid vacation, paid sick days and personal days off, depending on which client they work for. Each client has a contract with different provisions for such employee-compensated absences. It is impractical to estimate the amount of compensation for future absences, and, accordingly, no liability or receivable has been recorded in the accompanying financial statements. The Company's policy is to recognize the costs of compensated absences when actually paid to employees.
